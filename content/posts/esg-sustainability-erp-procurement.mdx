ESG writing often stops at frameworks. Sustainability work in a real company stops at **vendor procurement** and the ERP that already holds purchase orders, suppliers, and sites.

That is the product problem I care about.

## ESG is a data product

BRSR, GRI, and carbon accounting fail when they treat the report as the product. The product is the system that can answer "what did this vendor emit, this quarter, for this site?" I wrote about that in [ESG reporting as a product problem](/blog/esg-reporting-is-a-product-problem).

## Vendor procurement is the missing table

Supplier onboarding, purchase requests, and approvals are where Scope 3 starts. A sustainability team that cannot see procurement will invent a second database. A mobile engineer who has shipped ERP-shaped apps knows why that second database goes stale.

## Sustainability software still needs a phone

Plant managers and procurement teams do not live in a desktop BI tool. React Native is how those workflows leave the office. At GreensTurn I work on mobile surfaces around ESG and carbon. The same craft applies to an **ERP mobile app** that has to be trusted in the field.
