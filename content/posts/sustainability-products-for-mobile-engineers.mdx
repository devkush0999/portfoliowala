Sustainability work looks like policy from the outside. From the inside it is operations: meters, invoices, emission factors, site visits, and people who will not fill a 90-field form.

Building product in that world as a React Native developer changed what I think "good software" is.

## The user is often not at a desk

Carbon and ESG data is collected in plants, offices, farms, and supply chains. A phone is sometimes the right computer. That means poor networks, cameras, offline, and forms that can be completed in ten minutes, not ten days.

## Units and methodology are product features

A kilowatt-hour is not a vibe. If you hide the methodology, you cannot defend the number. Sustainability products have to show assumptions without drowning the user.

## Reduction is the point

Reporting is table stakes. The useful system helps someone cut energy, fix a leak, or choose a less carbon-intensive supplier. That is closer to a workflow tool than to a CMS.

I will keep writing from this intersection: mobile UX for field data, carbon accounting basics for engineers, and how sustainability products should feel as considered as a well-shipped consumer app.
